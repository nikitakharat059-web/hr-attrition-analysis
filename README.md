HR Attrition Analysis (Python + Power BI)
Analysis of employee attrition for 1,470 employees to find why people leave and which groups are most at risk, with an interactive Power BI dashboard and retention recommendations.

Dashboard

Problem Statement
Employee attrition is costly: companies lose skilled people and spend more on hiring and training. This project identifies the main drivers of attrition so that HR can act early.

Dataset
Source: IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance (public dataset on Kaggle)
Size: 1,470 employees, 35 columns
Note: This is a fictional dataset created by IBM data scientists for learning purposes. It is not real company data.
Tools Used
Python: pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn
Power BI Desktop: dashboard, DAX measures
GitHub: version control and publishing
Project Steps
Data cleaning: checked missing values and duplicates (none found), dropped columns with a single value (EmployeeCount, StandardHours, Over18).
Feature engineering: created AttritionFlag, AgeGroup, IncomeBand and TenureGroup.
Exploratory analysis: calculated attrition rate across departments, job roles, overtime, age, income, tenure, marital status, travel, job satisfaction and work-life balance.
Visualization: bar charts, pie chart, boxplot and correlation heatmap (saved in images/).
Dashboard: built an interactive Power BI dashboard with KPI cards, charts and slicers.
Key Findings
Metric	Result
Total employees	1,470
Employees who left	237
Overall attrition rate	16.12%
Overtime is the biggest driver. Employees working overtime leave at 30.5%, versus 10.4% for those who do not.
Low income means higher attrition. The lowest income band leaves at 29.3%, while the high income bands leave at about 10%.
New and young employees leave the most. Employees with 0-2 years at the company leave at 29.8%, and those aged 18-25 at 35.8%.
Some roles are far riskier. Sales Representatives have the highest attrition at 39.8%, followed by Laboratory Technicians at 23.9%. Research Directors (2.5%) and Managers (4.9%) are the most stable.
Sales is the riskiest department at 20.6%, compared with 13.8% in Research & Development.
Frequent travel and single status raise the risk. Frequent travellers leave at 24.9% (vs 8.0% for non-travellers), and single employees at 25.5% (vs about 10-12% for divorced and married employees).
Satisfaction matters. Employees with low job satisfaction leave at 22.8%, versus 11.3% for very high satisfaction.
Recommendations
Review the overtime policy and redistribute workload in high-overtime teams.
Revisit pay for low-income and high-risk roles, especially Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians.
Improve onboarding and mentoring during the first 2 years.
Reduce the travel burden or offer flexibility for frequent travellers.
Run regular job satisfaction and work-life balance surveys and act on low scores early.
Limitations
These factors overlap (for example, young employees are often also new and lower paid). The analysis shows which groups are at higher risk, not what causes people to leave.

Dashboard (Power BI)
KPI cards: Total Employees, Employees Left, Attrition Rate
Charts: attrition rate by overtime, department, job role, age group, income band and tenure
Donut chart: employees stayed vs left
Slicers: Department, Gender, JobRole
DAX measures used:

Total Employees = COUNTROWS(hr_attrition_cleaned)
Employees Left = SUM(hr_attrition_cleaned[AttritionFlag])
Attrition Rate = DIVIDE([Employees Left], [Total Employees], 0)

How to Run
Download the dataset from Kaggle and place the CSV in the project folder.
Install the libraries:
   pip install pandas matplotlib seaborn
Run the script:
   python hr_attrition_analysis.py
Open HR_Attrition_Dashboard.pbix in Power BI Desktop.
